Can Foreigners Buy Property in Albania?

Yes. Foreigners can purchase apartments, houses, and commercial property in Albania with nearly identical rights to Albanian citizens. No special government approval or pre-authorization is needed. Albania's foreign investment framework, anchored in Law No. 7764 of 1993, broadly recognizes foreign ownership of immovable property.

All you need is a valid passport and an Albanian tax number (NIPT) to get started.

What You Can and Can't Buy

No Restrictions

  • Apartments and residential units — anywhere in the country
  • Houses and villas
  • Commercial property (offices, shops, hotels)

Restrictions on Land

  • Foreigners cannot directly purchase agricultural land
  • Buying other types of land typically requires setting up an Albanian company (which can be 100% foreign-owned)
  • Long-term lease agreements of up to 99 years are available as an alternative for agricultural plots
  • Properties in coastal zones (within 200 meters of the shoreline) may fall under land restrictions even if they look like standard villas — always verify the cadastral classification

Your nationality does not affect what residential property you can buy. Albania treats all foreign nationals equally under the law.

The Buying Process: Step by Step

The entire process typically takes 2 to 8 weeks from accepted offer to final registration.

  1. Find a property — Work with a licensed local agent or browse listings online
  2. Agree on terms — Negotiate price (expect 6-8% below listing price)
  3. Hire a local lawyer — Essential for protecting your interests. Budget EUR 500-1,500
  4. Due diligence — Your lawyer checks the title at the State Cadastre Agency, verifies building permits, and confirms there are no liens or encumbrances
  5. Sign preliminary agreement — Typically with a 10% deposit
  6. Sign notarized purchase deed — Both parties sign before an official Albanian notary
  7. Register ownership — File with the National Cadastral Agency to complete the transfer

Important: A common pitfall is purchasing property built without proper permits or "legalized" under regularization programs that may still have restrictions. Always verify permits before signing anything.

Budget for 4-7% in total closing costs on top of the purchase price:

Albania's annual property tax of just 0.05% is one of the lowest in Europe. Compare that to 1-3% in most Western European countries.

Where to Buy: Best Locations

Tirana — The Capital

  • Prices: EUR 1,800-2,500/m² in central areas, up to EUR 5,000/m² in the exclusive Blloku district
  • 1-bedroom apartment: Around EUR 153,000 on average
  • Rental yields: 4.5-6.5%
  • Why buy: Year-round rental demand (not seasonal like the coast), fastest value growth at 12-15% annually, new international hotel brands (Hyatt, Hilton, IHG) arriving by 2026-2027
  • Best neighborhoods: Blloku, Liqeni Artificial, Komuna e Parisit

Saranda — The Riviera Hotspot

  • Prices: EUR 1,600-1,800/m² for new developments, EUR 2,200-3,000/m² for sea-view
  • Rental yields: 7-10% in prime locations
  • Peak season occupancy: 90%
  • Why buy: Highest rental yields in the country, booming tourism, airport expected by 2027 (projected to boost values 25-40%)
  • Caution: Highly seasonal — rentals may be vacant 6-8 months outside summer without good management

Vlora — The Rising Star

  • Prices: EUR 1,400-2,400/m², significantly lower than Tirana or Saranda
  • 1-bedroom apartment: EUR 120,000-180,000
  • Rental yields: 2.5-4.8%
  • Why buy: Brand-new Vlora International Airport opening in 2025-2026, major beachfront development underway, most affordable coastal option

Durres — The Affordable Option

  • Prices: EUR 1,200-1,900/m²
  • Why buy: Just 30 minutes from Tirana, EUR 2 billion Durres Yachts & Marina project backed by UAE developers, lowest entry point for coastal property

Ksamil — The Exclusive Resort

  • Prices: EUR 2,000-2,800/m²
  • Why buy: Albania's most famous beach destination, limited supply, premium tourist demand

Market Outlook: Is Now a Good Time to Buy?

Albania's property market has been one of Europe's strongest performers:

  • 18-26% annual price growth in 2024-2025 (vs. 3-4% EU average)
  • 12.47 million tourists visited Albania in 2025, up 6.6% year-on-year
  • EU accession negotiations are underway — Albania has opened 16 of 33 chapters. Historical data from Croatia and Bulgaria shows EU accession drives property prices up 40-100% over a decade
  • Growth is expected to moderate to 5-8% in 2026, making this potentially the last window before prices stabilize at higher levels

For comparison: sea-view apartments in Saranda cost EUR 2,200-3,000/m², while similar properties in Montenegro's Budva or Kotor already cost EUR 3,500-4,500/m². Albania still offers significant value.

Can You Get Residency Through Property?

Buying property in Albania does not automatically grant residency. However, owning property over 20 square meters supports a residence permit application under Law No. 79/2021.

There is no formal "golden visa" program. The standard route is:

  1. Purchase property (supports your application)
  2. Apply for a 1-year renewable residence permit
  3. After 5 years of legal residency, apply for permanent residency
  4. Eventually eligible for citizenship through naturalization

Financing Options

Albanian banks do offer mortgages to foreigners, though approval is less common:

  • Down payment: 25-40% (higher than for locals)
  • Requirements: Proof of stable income, local bank account, sometimes a co-borrower with Albanian ties
  • Reality: About 70% of foreign purchases are completed through direct bank transfers, not mortgages

Most foreign buyers pay cash or arrange financing in their home country.

Top Tips for Foreign Buyers

  1. Always hire a local lawyer — Don't rely on the seller's lawyer or the agent
  2. Verify the title at the Cadastre — This is non-negotiable. Title fraud exists
  3. Check building permits — Many properties were built without proper permits
  4. Visit in person at least once — Photos can be misleading
  5. Negotiate — Expect 6-8% below listing price
  6. Budget for all costs upfront — Transfer tax, notary, legal fees add 4-7%
  7. Consider seasonality — Coastal rentals drop sharply outside summer. Tirana offers year-round demand
  8. Open a local bank account early — Speeds up the buying process
  9. Get a power of attorney — If you can't be present for all steps, your lawyer can act on your behalf
  10. Don't rush — The market is growing, but making a bad purchase is worse than missing a deal

The Bottom Line

Albania is one of Europe's most accessible markets for foreign property buyers. Low barriers to entry, affordable prices, strong rental yields, and an EU accession trajectory make it compelling for investors and lifestyle buyers alike.

The window of opportunity is still open — prices remain a fraction of neighboring countries — but with 18-26% annual growth, that gap is closing fast.
